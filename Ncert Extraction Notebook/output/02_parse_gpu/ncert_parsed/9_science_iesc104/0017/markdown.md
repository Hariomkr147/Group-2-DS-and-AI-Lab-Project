As learnt in the earlier section, the displacement s of the object during the time interval t is given by the area enclosed within OABD. Thus,

$$
s =  area of OABD
$$

= area of the rectangle OACD + area of the triangle ABC

$$
\left( \mathrm{AO} \times \mathrm{DO} \right) + \left( \frac{1}{2} \times \mathrm{CA} \times \mathrm{BC} \right)
$$

Substituting $A 0 = u ,$ DO = CA = t and $\mathrm{BC} = \mathrm{BD} - \mathrm{CD} = \mathrm{EO} - \mathrm{AO} = ( \nu - \mu )$ we obtain

$$
s = u \times t + \frac { 1 } { 2 } \times \left[ t \times ( v - u ) \right]
$$

Substituting the value of (v – u) from Eq. (4.4a),

$$
\begin{aligned} &s = u t + \frac{1}{2} \times t \times a t^{2} \\&s = u t + \frac{1}{2} \times a t^{2}\\ \end{aligned}\tag{4.4b}
$$

These two equations (Eq. 4.4a and Eq. 4.4b) are the primary equations. Combining these equations in different ways, we can obtain three more equations. Let us derive one of those by eliminating t in Eq. (4.4b) (the remaining two equations are given as an exercise in ‘ The Journey Beyond’ at the end of the chapter).

From Eq. (4.4a), we get

$$
t = \frac{v - u}{a}
$$

Substituting this in Eq. (4.4b), we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}s &= u\left[\frac{\nu - u}{a}\right] + \frac{1}{2} \times a\left[\frac{\nu - u}{a}\right]^2 \\\overset{\longrightarrow}{\underset{\nu \to \nu}{\rightleftharpoons}} \quad & s = \frac{uv - u^2}{a} + \frac{u^2 + \nu^2 - 2uv}{2a} \\\overset{\longrightarrow}{\underset{\nu \to \nu}{\rightleftharpoons}} \quad & s = \frac{2uv - 2u^2 + u^2 + \nu^2 - 2uv}{2a} \\\overset{\longrightarrow}{\underset{\nu \to \nu}{\rightleftharpoons}} \quad & s = \frac{-u^2 + \nu^2}{2a} \\\overset{\longrightarrow}{\underset{\nu \to \nu}{\rightleftharpoons}} \quad & 2as = -u^2 + \nu^2 \\\overset{\longrightarrow}{\underset{\nu \to \nu}{\rightleftharpoons}} \quad & \nu^2 = u^2 + 2as\end{aligned}\tag{4.4c}
$$

For the motion of an object in a straight line with constant acceleration, the five physical quantities-displacement (s ), time interval (t), initial velocity (u), final velocity (v) and acceleration (a), can be related by the following set of equations,

$$
\upsilon = u + a t\tag{4.4a}
$$

$$
s = u t   +   { \frac { 1 } { 2 } } a t ^ { 2 }\tag{4.4b}
$$

$$
\nu ^ { 2 } = u ^ { 2 } + 2 a s\tag{4.4c}
$$

These are known as **kinematic equations**. These equations provide a mathematical description of how the motion of an object changes with time.
